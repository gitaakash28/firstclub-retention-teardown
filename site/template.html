The problem in 3 sentences

FirstClub wins customers on quality: freshness comes up in of its positive Play Store reviews. When an order goes wrong, the fix runs through a support chat, and a failure review that also complains about support signals the customer is leaving as often as one that doesn't ( vs ). As FirstClub scaled, the average rating of new written reviews fell from to between Jan–May and Jun–Oct 2026, so every slow fix now costs more.

How I did it

  1. Customer voice. I pulled all public Google Play reviews (June 2025 – October 2026). An LLM tagged the reviews with text by lifecycle stage (never ordered, first order, repeat) and theme, using a fixed taxonomy (prompt). On the 4 themes that can be checked against keywords, tags agreed 96–100% of the time; I also read every churn-flagged review by hand (check). This extends my Weekly Pulse agent.
  2. Day-1 SQL. I wrote the 8 Postgres queries I'd run in my first week against an assumed FirstClub schema. They cover cohorts, repeat rate after an issue by fix time, issue rate by order number, and an A/B readout with a confidence interval and guardrails. They run end to end on synthetic test data, and none of their output is claimed as a finding (queries).
  3. Decision. I wrote a PRD with an A/B test plan, RICE-ranked it against two alternatives (PRD), and built a clickable prototype of the flow.
  4. Keeping it current. A weekly GitHub Action pulls new reviews, tags them with the same taxonomy and refreshes a live pulse page. This page stays a fixed snapshot.

1. New reviews turned more critical as FirstClub scaled

Share of new written reviews rated 1–2★, by month (2026). Hover a bar for review count and average rating. The overall store rating is still 4.7 across 10,862 ratings. Written reviews skew critical, so the signal here is the trend, not the level.

2. Where the pain sits in the lifecycle

Negative and mixed reviews (≤3★) by the customer's stage. "Ordered" means the review describes a delivery or product, even when the reviewer doesn't say whether it was their first order.

30% of the pain comes before the first order: people find out the app doesn't serve their area only after they've signed up, or they get stuck on the OTP screen. That's a signup leak with a cheap fix. The retention problem is in the reviews from people who ordered.

"The app makes you enter all personal details and complete the entire setup, only to state at the very end that service is not available in my location. You should verify address/pincode serviceability at the very first step."1★, September 2026. The same complaint runs from October 2025 through September 2026.

3. What customers who leave talk about

Themes in the reviews where the customer says they uninstalled, stopped ordering or plan to leave. A review can carry up to 3 themes.

"If a packet of milk is not delivered and we try to raise it over chat, your team keeps us engaged for 30 minutes or more, repeating the same questions again and again."1★, June 2026
"First two orders were smooth and quality excellent… From the third one, it has been an ordeal… items missing."1★, May 2026

4. Why customers stay

Themes in the positive reviews.

Quality and freshness dominate the praise. The product promise works. Retention depends on protecting that promise when something slips.

"Good experience on my first delivery… I have seen many cases where things start going wrong after the first couple of deliveries. Only time will tell."5★, May 2026

6. The bet: make it right in one tap

What: for a customer's first 5 orders, a "Something not right?" button on the order card for 48 hours after delivery. The customer picks the items and gets an instant wallet credit, with no chat and no photo for fresh items under a cap. A small automatic credit also lands when delivery misses the promised ETA by more than 15 minutes. Every claim is tagged by SKU, batch and store and feeds the quality team.

Primary metric: among customers who report an issue in their first 5 orders, the share who order again within 14 days. Guardrails: credit cost as a percentage of GMV, claims per 100 orders, repeat claimers, and support contact rate.

Test: randomize 50/50 by customer at their first issue. With an assumed 40% baseline, detecting +8 pp needs 604 customers per arm (1,208 total) at α = 0.05 and 80% power (calculation). Run at least 2 full weeks and look at the result only at the planned end.

Try the clickable prototype

Is this already built? I checked FirstClub's own replies. Of FirstClub's developer replies to 1–2★ reviews from Aug–Oct 2026, ask the customer to email support with their order details, and none mention a self-serve credit or refund (script). In a walkthrough of the app on 26 Oct 2026, help was FAQs plus an AI assistant, and asking it for a refund started a back-and-forth (screenshots). So the concept gives that assistant a button: one tap on the order hands it the items and the problem. I had no past orders, so the order-level help path is the one thing I couldn't check.

Limitations

  • Play Store reviewers skew toward strong opinions, and a review is a stated reason, not observed behaviour. The first SQL query I'd run inside FirstClub is the real 14-day repeat rate after an early-order issue.
  • LLM tags are judgments. Theme tags were checked against keyword rules and I read every churn-flagged review, but they're still judgments; the prompt and every tag are public in this repo.
  • I walked through the app on 26 Oct 2026 but had no past orders, so I couldn't see whether a delivered order has its own report-an-issue path. That's the first thing I'd confirm.

Appendix: a US grocery benchmark

Why US data at all? FirstClub's order data isn't public, and reviews can't show behaviour. The open Instacart dataset (3.4M orders) is the largest public record of repeat grocery orders, so I used it for general grocery patterns and as a sample of my SQL. But it's US data from scheduled delivery, not 10-minute commerce in Bengaluru, and it has no churn label. Treat it as direction, never as FirstClub's numbers.
