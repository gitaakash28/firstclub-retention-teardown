The problem in 3 sentences

FirstClub wins customers on quality: freshness comes up in of its positive Play Store reviews. When an order goes wrong, the fix runs through a support chat, and a failure review that also complains about support signals the customer is leaving as often as one that doesn't ( vs ). As FirstClub scaled, the average rating of new written reviews fell from to between Jan–May and Jun–Oct 2026, so every slow fix now costs more.

How I did it

  1. Customer voice. I pulled all public Google Play reviews (June 2025 – October 2026). An LLM tagged the reviews with text by lifecycle stage (never ordered, first order, repeat) and theme, using a fixed taxonomy (prompt). On the 4 themes that can be checked against keywords, tags agreed 96–100% of the time; I also read every churn-flagged review by hand (check). This extends my Weekly Pulse agent.
  2. Day-1 SQL. I wrote the 8 Postgres queries I'd run in my first week against an assumed FirstClub schema. They cover cohorts, repeat rate after an issue by fix time, issue rate by order number, and an A/B readout with a confidence interval and guardrails. They run end to end on synthetic test data, and none of their output is claimed as a finding (queries).
  3. Decision. I wrote a PRD with an A/B test plan, RICE-ranked it against two alternatives (PRD), and built a clickable prototype of the flow.
  4. Keeping it current. A weekly GitHub Action pulls new reviews, tags them with the same taxonomy and refreshes a live pulse page. This page stays a fixed snapshot.

5. Beyond the first orders: the habit

Retention isn't only avoiding bad first orders. Customers who stay build a routine, and FirstClub's routine products are its milk subscriptions and small top-up orders.

Subscriptions are the daily habit, and opinion is split: 10 positive and 10 negative reviews. The negative ones describe specific, fixable failures: pause and resume not working, delivery timing, and, from one reviewer, a 25% overnight price rise. Those are engineering tickets, not a product bet, and they were twice as common per review in Jan–Jun as in Jul–Oct, so some may already be fixed. I'd check what's still broken before prioritizing. Fee complaints roughly doubled per review after July 2026 (still small numbers), which may mean a fee was introduced or raised then. I'd test whether the free-delivery minimum pushes small top-up orders to other apps (query) before anyone touches pricing.

7. The bet: protect the first five orders

Four principles, then three changes. Prevent before you compensate. Never hold a refund hostage. Trust the customer, verify with your own data. Give the existing AI a button.

A. Prevent: a first-orders quality lane. Packers get a flag on a customer's first 3 orders: best-in-crate produce, plus a minimum remaining shelf life on dairy and bakery (e.g. 3+ days), shown on product pages as a promise. Quality comes up in 33 of 76 first-order reviews.

B. Recover: "Something not right?" on the order card for 48 hours, on a customer's first 5 orders.

  • Approval: instant when FirstClub's own pick or pack data agrees (short-picked or substituted); otherwise one photo, still instant under the cap.
  • The customer chooses the remedy: a refund to the original payment method, an instant credit that never expires, or a free replacement in the next order.
  • Late delivery: 15+ minutes past the promised ETA refunds that order's delivery and handling fee automatically.
  • Repeat claimers go to FirstClub's existing AI assistant with the order already attached, and a person calls back.
  • Closing the loop: every claim is logged by SKU, batch and store, and the customer hears what changed.

C. Say where you deliver, early. Name the cities served in the store listing and ads, add a city waitlist, and check the pincode before asking for a phone number.

How to test B: randomize every new customer at their first order. The button itself changes who reports issues, so randomizing only the people who complain would compare different kinds of customers. Primary: the share of new customers with 3+ orders in their first 30 days. Pre-registered readout: 14-day reorder after a failure FirstClub's own data detects (late, short-picked), which fires the same way in both arms.

Size: 6,086–26,289 new customers per arm, depending on how many hit an issue (calculation). That's a few weeks at FirstClub's scale; run at least 4. Guardrails: credit cost as a percentage of GMV, claims per 100 orders, claims from accounts under 7 days old (the ₹100 welcome offer is an abuse risk), and promised delivery times must not creep up.

A is tested separately with a staggered store-by-store rollout, since it's an ops process, not a screen.

Limitations

  • Play Store reviewers skew toward strong opinions, and a review is a stated reason, not observed behaviour. The first SQL query I'd run inside FirstClub is the real 14-day repeat rate after an early-order issue.
  • LLM tags are judgments. Theme tags were checked against keyword rules and I read every churn-flagged review, but they're still judgments; the prompt and every tag are public in this repo.
  • I walked through the app on 26 Oct 2026 but had no past orders, so I couldn't see whether a delivered order has its own report-an-issue path. That's the first thing I'd confirm.

Appendix: a US grocery benchmark

Why US data at all? FirstClub's order data isn't public, and reviews can't show behaviour. The open Instacart dataset (3.4M orders) is the largest public record of repeat grocery orders, so I used it for general grocery patterns and as a sample of my SQL. But it's US data from scheduled delivery, not 10-minute commerce in Bengaluru, and it has no churn label. Treat it as direction, never as FirstClub's numbers.
